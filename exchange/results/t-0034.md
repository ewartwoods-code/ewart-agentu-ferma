## Executive conclusion

There is currently **no accessible first-party order export, buyer-address file, or GA4 property/report in this workspace**. Therefore no city, region, postcode, ZIP, or persona concentration can honestly be called a measured EWART WOODS result yet. The city lists below are **test-priority hypotheses**, not findings. The correct launch decision is to first build a privacy-safe order/GA4 geo table, then use external sources as cross-checks. Do not allocate materially more spend to a city merely because it is large, affluent, design-oriented, or appears in this report.

The strongest eventual evidence hierarchy is: **completed orders and contribution margin by purchaser geography > GA4 engaged sessions/conversions by geo > platform ad conversion data by geo > Google Trends directional interest > Meta/Similarweb/eRank/census proxies**.

## 1. Location/geo analysis method (first-party first)

### 1.1 Define the measurement unit and time windows

Use a rolling **24-month** history where available, plus trailing 90 days, trailing 30 days, and a comparable prior period. Keep an all-market table and separate market/language tables. A geo is eligible for advertising only when it has enough observations to avoid exposing individuals; use a minimum internal reporting cell (for example, 10 orders or 10 converting users) and suppress smaller cells. This is an operational privacy threshold, not a legal safe-harbour claim.

Primary KPIs per geo: orders, net revenue, gross margin/contribution after shipping and returns, AOV, repeat-order rate, refund rate, product/category mix, sessions, engaged sessions, add-to-cart, checkout, purchase, conversion rate, CAC/ROAS if ad spend exists. Rank **profit and conversion quality**, not orders alone.

### 1.3 Build the first-party geo table

1. Export order rows from Etsy, Amazon and Shopify for the same period. Keep a secure raw layer; analysis layer should contain a random order key, market/channel, date, SKU/category, net sales, shipping/returns/cost, and **aggregated geography**. Restrict raw addresses to authorised operations staff; do not upload names, full addresses, or postal codes to Meta/Google as a general audience list.
2. Normalise country, region, city, postcode/ZIP and language. Preserve original value plus a standardised value; handle UK outward/inward postcode rules, US ZIP+4 truncation only in the secure analysis layer, German/FR/LV/ES/NO postal formats, diacritics and address changes. Geocode only with a lawful processor and delete unnecessary full addresses after aggregation.
3. Join order geography to GA4 by market/country/city where possible. Never pretend an order address and GA4 user are the same person unless consented, deterministic first-party matching is lawful, and the join has been designed/approved. Use aggregate comparison instead: “orders concentrated in X; GA4 purchase events also elevated in X.”
4. Create `geo × channel × product_family × period` aggregates. Calculate orders/share, revenue/share, margin/share, AOV, CVR and repeat rate. Use confidence intervals or at least sample-size flags; avoid ranking tiny cells.
5. Cross-check each candidate geo against (a) GA4 purchase/engaged sessions, (b) Google Ads/Meta geo performance, (c) Trends, and (d) census context. Label the result: **Confirmed** (orders + independent first-party analytics agree), **Supported** (orders plus one corroborator), **Directional** (external sources only), or **Unknown**.
6. Run a holdout test: split each market into geographically coherent cells, maintain a control or lower-spend cell, use consistent creative/landing page/offer, and judge incremental contribution margin after a full conversion window. Do not use last-click alone to crown a geo.

### 1.4 ZIP/postal precision rules

- Postal/ZIP-level advertising is only usable where Google/Meta actually offers that target and the service area is large enough. “Order address available” does **not** mean the platform will accept or resolve that postcode.
- Start at country → region/metro → city/postal sector, then narrow only when a cell has enough orders/conversions and profitable delivery economics. For UK, test outward-code districts/sectors only if the platform supports the exact target; for US, test 5-digit ZIPs, never publish individual addresses. For DE/FR/ES/LV/NO, verify platform availability and use radius/municipality alternatives when postal targeting is missing.
- Suppress tiny cells and sensitive categories. Use aggregate reports, consented hashed first-party audience workflows, retention limits and documented lawful basis. Obtain legal review before using customer lists or location-based profiling under GDPR/UK GDPR/US state privacy laws.

## 3. Precision targeting build suggestions

### 3.1 Google Ads: recommended build

**Campaign structure:** separate country/language and major product family; do not combine US, UK, DE, FR, LV, ES and NO in one budget/learning pool. Start with Shopping/PMax only after feed, shipping and local currency/language are correct; maintain a controlled Search campaign for high-intent queries (e.g., local equivalents of “handmade wooden floating shelf”, “wood wall shelf”, “wooden dog bowl stand”). EWART’s internal Google Ads skill correctly prioritises feed quality, product grouping and measured search-term negatives.

**Geo layers:**
- Campaign 1: whole country (baseline), location option set to people **in** the target location rather than broad “interested in” traffic where available.
- Campaign 2: top measured metro/region cells; separate budgets so a national campaign cannot absorb all spend.
- Campaign 3: postal/ZIP or radius cells only after a measured signal. Google documents country-specific target availability, including postal codes, cities, regions and more: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722075.
- Radius: use 10–25 km around a measured city/fulfilment-relevant metro as the initial test; expand to 40–80 km only if delivery economics and conversion support it. These are **test settings, not discovered optimal radii**.
- Exclude locations where shipping cost, delivery time, returns or margin make the product unprofitable. Check location report and user-location versus geo-targeted location dimensions before decisions.

**Audiences/signals (observation first where possible):** in-market home decor/home furnishings, home improvement, gifts/seasonal shoppers, pet supplies or baby/kids only for relevant product families; custom segments built from high-intent search terms/competitor URLs; first-party GA4 purchasers/qualified visitors where consent and Google policy permit; customer match only with lawful notice/consent and minimum-list rules. Treat audience signals as expansion hints, not hard proof of purchase.

**Demographics:** test age/household status/parental status only where available and relevant; do not exclude by gender or age based on stereotypes. Let conversion data justify bid adjustments. Avoid sensitive inferences.

**Measurement:** import Shopify/Etsy/Amazon-appropriate conversions only through lawful, consented, deduplicated paths; use `purchase` with value/currency, transaction ID, and refund handling. Report geo at country/region/city and only publish ZIP rows above threshold. Use contribution-margin ROAS, not revenue ROAS alone.

### 3.2 Meta: recommended build

**Locations:** country ad set first; then city/region/postal and radius ad sets where the interface/API resolves them. Meta’s Marketing API targeting documentation explicitly describes `geo_locations` for country, region, city and ZIP and city radius examples: https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/targeting. Use a 10–25 km city radius as a controlled hypothesis, not an assumption. Separate prospecting and retargeting; exclude purchasers from prospecting where policy and consent allow.

**Interests/creative:** use relevant, currently available interests such as interior design, home decor, furniture, woodworking/handmade, Scandinavian/Nordic design, gardening/houseplants, pets, parenting, wedding/gifting; verify each interest in the live account because availability changes. Keep one broad/Advantage+ audience test against one interest-led test; do not stack so tightly that delivery collapses. Localise copy/creative by language and product intent; show real scale, installation, timber/metal detail and delivery.

**Lookalikes/custom audiences:** create a high-quality purchaser value list from Shopify/consented first-party data, preferably 180–365 days and enough volume per market; separate markets/languages and product families if sample size supports it. Test 1% lookalike first, then 1–3% only if incremental quality holds. Use website/GA4 audiences through Meta Pixel + Conversions API only with consent and correct data-processing configuration. Never upload raw addresses or unconsented customer data. Meta custom-audience docs: https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences/guides/custom-audiences; overview: https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/audiences.

**Testing:** each market: broad geo prospecting vs interest-led vs purchaser lookalike (if eligible), one creative variable at a time; optimise to purchase/value, not link clicks. Use geo split tests and report reach, frequency, CPM, CTR, ATC, purchase CVR, CPA, contribution margin and incrementality.

## 5. Minimum data request to finalise the city/ZIP recommendations

Required from the owner/admin (read-only exports are enough):

1. Etsy, Amazon and Shopify order exports for the last 24 months with order date, destination country/region/city/postal field (masked/aggregated where necessary), SKU, quantity, net revenue, shipping, refund and platform fees.
2. GA4 property access or CSV/API exports for the same periods: country, region, city, sessions, engaged sessions, add-to-cart, begin checkout, purchase, revenue, source/medium/campaign, device; confirm consent mode and reporting identity.
3. Google Ads and Meta geo reports: impressions, clicks, spend, conversions/value, campaign, country/region/city/postal/radius, and location-option settings.
4. Product-level shipping cost, delivery time, return rate and contribution margin by destination.
5. Consent/legal confirmation before any Customer Match, Meta Custom Audience, Pixel/CAPI, or other activation. Aggregate analysis can proceed without uploading PII.

**Final output after access:** a ranked table for every market with `geo`, `postal/ZIP if supported`, orders, margin, AOV, GA4 purchase corroboration, ad CVR/CPA/ROAS, sample size, source agreement, freshness, confidence, and recommended action (`scale`, `test`, `exclude`, `unknown`).
