## The three findings that matter

**1. The catalogues share no key.** Of 342 Amazon.de listings, 306 use Amazon-generated SKUs
(`XX-XXXX-XXXX`) and only 5 use Shopify's `123_NAME_` convention. There is no SKU, and no barcode
field populated on both sides, that links a Shopify product to an Amazon ASIN. The matching in
`product-master.csv` is therefore title-token overlap against the **UK** catalogue (chosen because
its titles are English, like Shopify's), scored and labelled by confidence: 121 high, 25 likely,
102 none.

This is the finding with the most leverage. Until a shared identifier exists, every future
cross-channel report — coverage, price parity, stock, profit-by-channel — inherits this same
guesswork.

**2. 78 active products (38%) have no identifiable Amazon listing.** Two coherent clusters rather
than random stragglers: the **metal shelving/decor range** (Corner Shelves, Perforated Display and
Folded, Wall Shelf FACE, Nursery Bookshelves, Shoe Rack CAVALLO) and **free-standing furniture**
(Buffet Cabinet, Living Room Table, coffee tables, the CAVALLO hallway pieces). Amazon's catalogue
is dominated by wooden floating shelves and toilet-paper holders.

Stated honestly: "up to 78, and the first job is to check which." Some are certainly on Amazon
under titles the matcher could not connect, and one of them is the Gift Card, which should not be
there at all.

**3. Two marketplaces are fully stocked and completely unadvertised.** Amazon.pl holds 320
listings and Amazon.ie 299 — and both spent **€0.00** on ads in the last 30 days. Meanwhile Saudi
Arabia and UAE are connected with zero listings. Whether that is strategy or drift is Hermes' call;
the data layer only reports it.

## Data-quality problems found while building

Six, listed in `inventory-coverage-report.md`. The two that will bite soonest:

- **36 products have no Shopify SKU**, including live sellers (`Metal Shoe Rack CAVALLO`,
  `Plant Stand CAVALLO`, `Vinyl Record Shelf - Brass Collection`, `Floating Shelf CurveLine`).
- **12 SKU values are shared by two or more different products** — `56_SOFA_PH1 _` across four Sofa
  arm tray colours, `57_STEP_WHPA_` across three Step Stools, `334_FLOA_WALN_` across two products
  that are not the same thing. Any stock or profit logic keyed on SKU will merge them silently.

## What the data layer now gives us — in plain words

Before this task, questions about the business had to be answered by opening four different
websites. Now they are answered by reading files in one folder:

- **"Where is this product actually being sold?"** One line in `product-master.csv` per product,
  with its Amazon ASIN and Google status.
- **"What are we NOT selling on Amazon?"** A concrete list of 78 products, not a feeling.
- **"How big is the business right now?"** 248 products, 206 live, 46 Shopify orders and $5,730 in
  the last 30 days, 12 Amazon marketplaces with ~300–370 listings each.
- **"What changed since last time?"** Nothing yet — but every file carries a capture date and lives
  in git, so the next capture can be diffed against this one. That is the real payoff, and it
  starts accruing from today.

And one thing it deliberately does **not** give us: any Etsy data. There is no connector, so the
`etsy` column is empty for all 248 rows. That is a hole in the data layer, not a finding about Etsy.
